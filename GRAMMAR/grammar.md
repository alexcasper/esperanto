# La Gramatiko de Esperanto — A Grammar Guide

*Milestone GRAMMAR · SKL-8m1r.4 · evidence base: `CORPUS/`, 131 normalized
sources (1887–1929), searched with `tools/find_examples.py`*

Every claim below that can be checked against usage is followed by a citation
in the form *quoted text* — `source:line`, or by a frequency count. Where the
corpus contradicts or complicates a rule, §6 says so rather than leaving the
rule looking cleaner than the evidence.

## 1. Fundamentoj — Fundamentals

Esperanto has no grammatical irregularity. These are the sixteen rules of the
*Fundamento de Esperanto* (Zamenhof, 1905), which is unchangeable
(*neŝanĝebla*): later usage extends the language, it does not overturn them.

1. **No indefinite article; `la` is the only article, and is invariable.** It
   does not inflect for number, gender or case.
2. **Nouns end in `-o`; the plural adds `-j`.** *libro* → *libroj*.
3. **Adjectives end in `-a` and agree with their noun in number and case.**
   *belaj libroj*, *belajn librojn*.
4. **Numerals are invariable**: unu, du, tri, kvar, kvin, ses, sep, ok, naŭ,
   dek, cent, mil.
5. **Personal pronouns**: mi, vi, li, ŝi, ĝi, ni, ili, si (reflexive);
   possessives add `-a`: mia, via, sia.
6. **Verbs do not conjugate for person or number.** `-i` infinitive, `-as`
   present, `-is` past, `-os` future, `-us` conditional, `-u` imperative.
7. **Adverbs end in `-e`**; comparison with *pli … ol* and *plej*.
8. **Prepositions govern the nominative.**
9. **Every word is read as written**, one letter to one sound (§4).
10. **Stress falls on the penultimate vowel**: *li-BRO*, *es-pe-RAN-to*.
11. **Compounds are formed by joining roots**, head-final, with an optional
    linking vowel: *skribotablo* (writing-table).
12. **Negation is single.** One negative word negates the clause; a second
    does not reinforce it (§6.2).
13. **The accusative `-n` marks motion towards** as well as the direct object:
    *iri Parizon*, *en la ĉambron* (into the room) against *en la ĉambro*
    (in the room).
14. **Every preposition has a fixed meaning**; where none fits, the indefinite
    *je* is used.
15. **International words are adopted with Esperanto spelling and endings.**
16. **The final `-o` of a noun, and the `-a` of the article, may be elided**
    and replaced by an apostrophe. Both are attested: *Al la sanktej' kvieta
    de l' diino* — `pg-22592:65`. Article elision is common in the corpus —
    2027 lines contain *de l'* — and noun elision is largely poetic.

## 2. Morfologio — Morphology

A word is [prefix…] root [suffix…] ending. Every morpheme is an independent
word-forming element, and derivation is fully productive: the affix inventory
below is the main reason a small root stock covers a large vocabulary.

| Affix | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **mal-** | direct opposite | *bona* good → *malbona* bad |
| **re-** | again, back | *relegi* reread |
| **bo-** | relation by marriage | *bopatrino* mother-in-law |
| **dis-** | apart, in all directions | *disigi* separate |
| **ek-** | sudden or inceptive | *ekridi* burst out laughing |
| **eks-** | former | *eksprezidento* ex-president |
| **fi-** | moral disapproval | *fivorto* foul word |
| **ge-** | both sexes together | *gepatroj* parents |
| **pra-** | ancestral, primeval | *prahomo* early human |
| **-aĉ-** | physical contempt | *domaĉo* hovel |
| **-ĉj- / -nj-** | affectionate names (m./f.) | *paĉjo* dad, *panjo* mum |
| **-in-** | female | *patrino* mother |
| **-id-** | offspring | *reĝido* prince |
| **-ul-** | person characterised by | *junulo* youth |
| **-ej-** | place for | *lernejo* school |
| **-il-** | instrument | *tranĉilo* knife |
| **-aĵ-** | concrete thing | *manĝaĵo* food |
| **-ec-** | abstract quality | *beleco* beauty |
| **-ad-** | continued or repeated action | *legado* reading |
| **-ar-** | collective | *arbaro* forest, *vortaro* dictionary |
| **-er-** | single particle | *sablero* grain of sand |
| **-uj-** | container, country (§6.1) | *cigaredujo*, *Francujo* |
| **-ist-** | occupation, adherent | *instruisto* teacher |
| **-an-** | member, inhabitant | *klubano* club member |
| **-em-** | disposition | *laborema* industrious |
| **-ind-** | worthy of | *leginda* worth reading |
| **-ebl-** | possible to | *legebla* legible |
| **-ig-** | causative, make | *beligi* beautify |
| **-iĝ-** | become | *fariĝi* become |
| **-obl- / -on- / -op-** | multiple / fraction / collective numeral | *duobla*, *duono*, *duope* |
| **-um-** | indeterminate relation | *kolumo* collar |
| **-ant- / -int- / -ont-** | active participles | *leganta, leginta, legonta* |
| **-at- / -it- / -ot-** | passive participles | *legata, legita, legota* |

**Compound tenses** combine *esti* with a participle: *li estis rigardeginta
Alicion kun evidenta scivolemo* — `pg-17482:1813`. Participles in `-e` form
adverbial clauses: *leginte la leteron, li foriris* (having read the letter,
he left).

## 3. Sintakso — Syntax

### Word order

**Word order is free; SVO is the default.** Because the accusative marks the
object, role is carried by case rather than position, and object-initial
clauses are ordinary rather than marked: *Mian pekon mi konfesis al Vi* —
`pg-11307:246`; *La voĉdonon mi proponis sole por tio* — `pg-20006:316`. The
pattern "object in `-n` before the subject pronoun" occurs on 847 lines of the
corpus, so this is normal prose, not poetic licence.

Adjectives may precede or follow their noun; preposed is the neutral prose
order.

### Questions

*Ĉu* + a declarative clause forms a yes/no question, with no inversion:
*Ĉu vi venas?* Interrogative correlatives stay in place: *Kiu venis?*

### La tabelvortoj — the correlatives

Forty-five words on one grid of nine endings by five series. The ending
carries the meaning, the initial the series.

| ending | i- (indefinite) | ki- (interrogative / relative) | ti- (demonstrative) | ĉi- (universal) | neni- (negative) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **-o** thing | io | kio | tio | ĉio | nenio |
| **-u** individual | iu | kiu | tiu | ĉiu | neniu |
| **-a** kind | ia | kia | tia | ĉia | nenia |
| **-es** possession | ies | kies | ties | ĉies | nenies |
| **-e** place | ie | kie | tie | ĉie | nenie |
| **-am** time | iam | kiam | tiam | ĉiam | neniam |
| **-al** reason | ial | kial | tial | ĉial | nenial |
| **-el** manner | iel | kiel | tiel | ĉiel | neniel |
| **-om** quantity | iom | kiom | tiom | ĉiom | neniom |

`-al` is reason and `-el` is manner, not the reverse: *Kial vi malĝojas, mia
animo* — `pg-11307:551` (why); *li estos kiel arbo, plantita apud akvaj
torentoj* — `pg-11307:26` (how, as). The `-es` row is a full member of the
paradigm: *kies folio ne velkas* — `pg-11307:27`.

The correlatives take `-j` and `-n` where sense allows (*tiujn*, *kiujn*).

### Clauses

- Subordination with *ke* (that), *ĉu* (whether), *se* (if).
- Purpose with *por ke* + the imperative `-u`: *por ke eniru la reĝo de
  gloro* — `pg-11307:167`.
- Relative clauses with *kiu*, *kio*, *kies*.
- The reflexive *si* refers only to a third-person subject: *kiam li ŝajnigis
  sin freneza* — `pg-11307:273`. First and second person use *min*, *vin*.

## 4. Fonologio kaj Ortografio

One letter, one sound (rule 9). Five pure vowels **a e i o u**. Consonants as
in English except **c** = *ts*, **ĉ** = *ch* in *church*, **ĝ** = *j* in
*jam*, **ĥ** = *ch* in Scots *loch*, **ĵ** = *s* in *measure*, **ŝ** = *sh*,
and **ŭ**, which is non-syllabic and occurs in the diphthongs *aŭ* and *eŭ*.

Stress is always penultimate (rule 10). Vowels in hiatus each count as a
syllable, so *radio* is *ra-DI-o*.

### ASCII substitutes

Two conventions replace the diacritics where they cannot be typed, and both
occur in this corpus, which matters when reading the sources directly:

- **x-system** — `cx gx hx jx sx ux`. Used by 11 of the 131 sources; *x* is
  not an Esperanto letter, so the mapping is unambiguous.
- **h-system** — `ch gh hh jh sh`, with `ŭ` written as plain `u`. This is the
  Fundamento's own fallback. It is ambiguous, because `gh` and `ch` also
  arise legitimately across morpheme boundaries in compounds such as
  *flughaveno* (flug+haveno), and because the breve is simply lost. One
  source (`pg-32035`) is written in it.

`tools/normalize_corpus.py` converts both to diacritics before the text
reaches `CORPUS/`, gating each conversion on the file rather than applying it
blindly.

## 5. Pragmatiko kaj Registro

- Greetings and thanks: *Saluton*, *Ĝis (la) revido*, *Dankon*,
  *Bedaŭrinde*. *Bonvolu* + infinitive softens a request.
- **No T/V distinction**: *vi* addresses everyone. Politeness is carried by
  phrasing — *Ĉu vi povus …?*, *Mi ŝatus …* — not by pronoun choice.
- Register: technical prose favours SVO with preposed adjectives; verse
  reorders freely, which rule 3 permits because case is marked.
- Word-formation economy is a stylistic preference of the classical writers:
  build from existing roots (*malsanulejo*) rather than borrow (*hospitalo*),
  though both are attested.
- Zamenhof's own prose, collected in `RAW/wsdump-Zamenhof_Dietterle_Originala_
  Verkaro_1929.txt`, is the usual style reference.

## 6. Uzado — where the corpus complicates the rule

The corpus is period material (1887–1929). Grammars often describe Esperanto
as a system of perfectly symmetric rules, but real literary and period usage
reveals clear statistical preferences, stylistic conventions, and historical
shifts. The subsections below document how classical texts handle these
nuances, each substantiated by corpus frequencies and direct citations.

### 6.1 Country names: `-ujo` dominates, not `-io`

Rule 15 and the `-uj-` suffix give *Francujo*, *Anglujo*. The corpus is
lopsided in their favour: *Francujo* 172 occurrences against *Francio* 1;
*Anglujo* 95 against *Anglio* 4.

- *Napoleonidoj esperas ricevi la tronon de Francujo* — `pg-20006:624`
- *imperiestro de Aŭstrujo kaj la reĝo de Francio* — `pg-23774:1269`

Modern Esperanto has largely moved to `-io`. A reader of these historical texts
should expect `-ujo`; a writer of modern Esperanto should not conclude from the
corpus that `-io` is rare today.

### 6.2 Negation is single, not doubled

One negative word suffices and is not reinforced by *ne*. Negative
correlatives appear on 5965 lines; the pattern *ne* followed closely by a
`neni-` word appears on 10, and those are mostly contrastive rather than
genuine double negation.

- *Mi ripetas, mi nenion vidis* — `pg-28968:3515` (I repeat, I saw nothing — not *mi ne vidis nenion*)

### 6.3 Compound tenses are rarer than their prominence suggests

The *esti* + participle construction is fully grammatical and attested:

- *li estis rigardeginta Alicion kun evidenta scivolemo* — `pg-17482:1813`

However, simple tenses carry the vast majority of narrative in the corpus.
Compound tenses are reserved for explicit aspectual contrasts (such as past
action before another past event) rather than serving as default narrative
tenses.

### 6.4 Demonstrative particle *ĉi*: preposed vs postposed

Textbooks frequently teach *ĉi tiu* / *ĉi tie* as standard, but the corpus
shows that both preposed and postposed *ĉi* are thoroughly established.
Overall, *tiu ĉi* (3901 lines) and *ĉi tiu* (3983 lines) are split almost
evenly. For neuter *tio*, postposed *tio ĉi* (1674 lines) strongly outnumbers
preposed *ĉi tio* (587 lines). For place, preposed *ĉi tie* (2636 lines) leads
over *tie ĉi* (1108 lines).

- *ĉu tiu ĉi lando estas Nov-Zelando aŭ Aŭstralio?* — `pg-17482:261`
- *En ĉi tiu momento la dompordo malfermiĝis* — `pg-17482:1487`
- *Ŝi kredis ke tio ĉi certe estas _lia_ domo* — `pg-17482:1767`
- *Mabelo, mi restos ĉi tie, kaj neniom utilos* — `pg-17482:490`

### 6.5 Dialogue tags and Verb-Subject inversion

While default declarative order is SVO, reported speech tags in narrative
heavily invert to Verb-Subject (VS) order when the subject is a full noun phrase.
The construction `diris la [noun]` appears on 1977 lines, compared to only 295
lines for `la [noun] diris` (a nearly 7:1 ratio favoring inversion).

- *Pardonu," diris la Muso tre ĝentile* — `pg-17482:648`
- *Mi petas pardonon, estimata sinjorino," diris la nekonato* — `pg-18178:394`

### 6.6 Passive participles: ongoing action (`-ata`) vs resulting state (`-ita`)

The distinction between imperfective/durative `-ata` (action in progress) and
perfective/resultative `-ita` (action completed, resulting state) is strictly
observed in the corpus. For instance, *estas fermita* (is in a closed state)
occurs on 41 lines, whereas *estas fermata* (is in the process of being closed)
occurs on 3 lines.

- *La kunveno estas fermita.* — `pg-19803:4792`
- *kubuto, kaj la pordo estas fermata kaj ŝlosata.* — `pg-49232:506`

### 6.7 Adverbial accusative of duration and time point

The accusative ending `-n` regularly marks duration of time and specific points
in time without requiring prepositions like *dum* or *je*.

- *oni ne povas tie ĉi atendi la tutan tagon.* — `pg-17482:2748`
- *la tutan tagon, ofte la tutan nokton* — `pg-17665:250`

### 6.8 Directional accusative after locative prepositions

Prepositions that primarily denote location (*en*, *sur*, *sub*, *antaŭ*) take
the accusative `-n` when they express movement into or onto a location.
Across the corpus, *en la [noun]o* (location: 13,219 lines) contrasts with
*en la [noun]on* (destination: 3,842 lines). In contrast, prepositions that
are inherently directional (*al*, *ĝis*, *el*, *de*, *tra*) govern the nominative
(*al la [noun]o*: 9,511 lines; *al la [noun]on*: virtually 0 valid instances).

- *Li venis en la ĉambron tra* — `pg-17482:3286` (motion into room)
- *La vidaĵo en la ĉambro de la kuiristo restadis* — `pg-11511:1137` (location in room)
- *NORA. Dankon; metu ĝin sur la tablon.* — `pg-19030:1664` (motion onto table)

### 6.9 Correlative conjunction pairs: *kaj … kaj*, *aŭ … aŭ*, *nek … nek*

Coordinating conjunctions are systematically doubled to mark correlative pairs:
*kaj … kaj* (both … and, 1,141 lines), *nek … nek* (neither … nor, 252 lines),
and *aŭ … aŭ* (either … or, 202 lines).

- *vi? Jes, vi efektive aspektas kaj laca kaj dormema.* — `pg-19030:3291`
- *Ĉar ŝi kapablis respondi nek unu nek alian* — `pg-17482:276`
- *nepre estos aŭ rosmaro aŭ hipopotamo* — `pg-17482:536`

### 6.10 Qualitative (*tia*) vs demonstrative (*tiu*) correlatives

The distinction between quality/kind (*tia* = such a, that kind of) and
individual identity (*tiu* = that specific one) is consistently maintained with
nouns (*tiu [noun]* 9,740 lines vs *tia [noun]* 1,999 lines).

- *kun tia homo, kaj doni al li ian promeson!* — `pg-19030:1492`
- *ke la verko de _unu homo_ ne povas esti senerara, se tiu homo eĉ* — `pg-20006:246`

### 6.11 Distributive preposition *po*

Rule 14 governs prepositions with fixed meanings. *Po* is uniquely
distributive, expressing "at the rate of / apiece", governing a numeral or
quantity.

- *ĉiu flanko po unu* — `pg-17482:3065`
- *sur ambaŭ flankoj brulas po ses grandaj* — `pg-18178:1489`

### 6.12 Adverbial participles in `-e` and subject alignment

Participles functioning adverbially in `-e` (*vidinte*, *sciante*) modify the
subject of the main clause in which they reside.

- *Vidinte tion la ceteraj sekvis ŝin.* — `pg-18178:1899`
- *Ne ĉiuj povas, kiel mi, forgesi, ke ili estas homoj* — `pg-25311:571`

### 6.13 Indefinite preposition *je*

When no specific spatial or logical preposition applies (Rule 14), *je* serves
as the indefinite preposition. In corpus usage, it appears primarily with
fullness/repletion (*plena je*), physical contact/grasping (*preni je la mano*),
and indeterminate time expressions.

- *longo plena je fumo.* — `pg-17482:1522`
- *preninte la knabaĉon je la mano, trenis lin al la fenestro* — `pg-18178:3331`

### 6.14 Note on evidence

Frequencies and counts above come from `tools/find_examples.py --compare` and
automated regex searches over the Esperanto-language literary and historical
sources in `CORPUS/`; English-language grammars (`pg-7787`, `pg-8177`, `pg-16967`)
and multilingual tables are excluded to prevent skewed counts.
